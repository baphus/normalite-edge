## 1. Getting in

1. Go to the Normalite EDGE address your instructor gave you.
2. Click **Sign in with Google**.
3. Sign in with your **`@cnu.edu.ph`** school Google account.

That is all. There is no separate registration form, no password to create, and **no waiting for approval**.

### Important

- **You must use your `@cnu.edu.ph` account.** Personal Gmail will not work. This is how the system knows you are a CNU student.
- **You have no Normalite EDGE password.** You sign in with Google. So there is nothing to forget, and no password to reset.
- **If Google will not let you in**, that is a problem with your school Google account, not with Normalite EDGE. Contact CNU IT or your instructor.
- **If Google lets you in but Normalite EDGE shows you nothing**, your account has not been set up yet. Tell your instructor or the administrator.

### First time in

You will be asked to complete your profile — your name, campus, program, year level, and section.

**Get your program right.** Your program decides which exams and study decks you can see. If it is wrong, you will not see the material meant for you. If you notice it is wrong later, ask your instructor or an administrator to fix it — you cannot change it yourself.

There is also a short guided tour on your first visit. It is worth the two minutes.

## 2. Your dashboard

Your home screen. It shows:

- Exams available to you now
- Your recent results
- Your performance over time
- **A daily practice question** — one question, every day
- Upcoming conferences and calendar events
- Achievements

**On the daily question:** answer it every time you sign in. It takes fifteen seconds. Small daily contact with the material beats a long session once a week, and this is the least effort the system will ever ask of you.

## 3. Study Hub

`Study Hub` — where you learn the material. Study decks are sets of questions with answers and explanations.

### Three modes, three purposes

| Mode | What happens | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| **View** | Question and answer shown together | First meeting the material, or revising quickly |
| **Flashcard** | Question first — you think, then reveal the answer | Testing whether you actually know it |
| **Quiz** | You answer and get scored | Practising under a bit of pressure |

**Use all three, in that order.** View to learn it, flashcard to check you know it, quiz to prove it.

### Be honest in flashcard mode

The temptation is to reveal the answer the moment it feels familiar, think "yes, I knew that", and move on. That feeling is not knowledge — it is recognition, and recognition disappears in an examination hall.

Commit to an answer in your head *before* you reveal. If you were wrong, you have just learned something valuable. If you only felt like you knew it, you have learned nothing.

### Your progress is saved

You can stop mid-session and pick it up later. Nothing is lost if you close the browser.

### Your own decks

You can create your own custom decks — for the topics you personally keep getting wrong. Private to you.

**This is the most underused feature in the system.** A deck of only the questions you have got wrong is worth several times a deck of everything, because it spends all its time on your actual weaknesses instead of the material you already know.

### Cannot see a deck you expected?

Decks are assigned to programs. If a deck is not visible, it is probably not assigned to yours — or your program is recorded incorrectly. Ask your instructor.

## 4. Taking an exam

`Exams` → choose an exam → start

### Before you start

**Read this section before your first exam. Not during it.**

- **The clock does not stop.** Once you start, the timer runs. Closing the browser does not pause it. Losing your internet does not pause it. Going to eat does not pause it. The time is calculated on the server, not on your device.
- **Check how long you have** and how many questions there are, before you begin.
- **Start when you are actually ready.** Somewhere with working internet and no interruptions.
- **Charge your device.**
- **Exams have a schedule.** You can only start inside the open window. Do not leave it to the final ten minutes — if something goes wrong, there will be no time left to recover.

### During the exam

- Each section has its own time limit. Watch the countdown.
- **Your answers save automatically every 15 seconds.** You do not need to save anything yourself.
- You can move between questions.
- The time you spend on each question is recorded. This is not a penalty; it just gives your instructor useful information.

### If something goes wrong mid-exam

**Do not panic, and do not start over.**

Your answers were saved up to the last automatic save — at most 15 seconds of work is at risk. Sign back in and resume the attempt. Everything you had answered will be there, as long as the exam window and your time have not run out.

**Remember that your clock kept running while you were away.** If your internet drops for ten minutes, you have lost ten minutes of exam time. Get back in as fast as you can.

### Submitting

Submit when you are finished. If time runs out first, the exam **submits itself automatically** with everything you had answered. You will not lose your work by running out of time — you will only lose the questions you never reached.

## 5. Rules during an exam

Depending on your institution's settings, these may apply. Assume they do.

### Do not switch tabs or leave the exam window

The system can detect when you leave the exam window — switching browser tabs, minimising, or moving to another app. There is a short grace period for accidents, but leaving the window is recorded as a violation and reported to your instructor.

**Practically: close everything else before you start.** Messaging apps, other tabs, anything that shows a pop-up notification. A notification that steals focus can be recorded as a violation even though you did nothing wrong — and while your instructor can look into it, the simplest solution is to remove the risk beforehand.

### Attempt limits and cooldowns

Some exams limit how many times you may take them, and may require a waiting period between attempts. Check before you start. **Do not treat your first attempt as a trial run** — it may be your only one.

### The obvious one

These are mock examinations designed to tell you how ready you are for the LET. A score you obtained by looking things up tells you nothing about how you will perform in an examination hall where you cannot. You would be the only person harmed by it.

## 6. Your results

After submitting, you can see:

- Your score
- **Every question, your answer, the correct answer, and the explanation**
- How long you spent per question
- Your performance across all attempts over time

Depending on the exam's settings, you may see feedback immediately as you answer, or only after you submit. Your instructor decides this.

### Read the explanations. All of them.

Not just the ones you got wrong.

The explanation on a question you answered correctly tells you *whether you were right for the right reason*. Guessing correctly and knowing the answer look identical on a score sheet and are completely different in an examination.

**And on the ones you got wrong** — the explanation is the single most valuable thing in this system. It is precisely targeted at a gap you have just proved you have. Ten minutes reading explanations properly is worth more than an hour of rereading notes you already understand.

### Watch the trend

Look at your performance over time, not at one score. One bad result after a poor night's sleep means very little. A steady downward drift across several attempts means something, and so does steady improvement from a low start.

## 10. Problems and answers

### "I cannot sign in with Google"

A problem with your `@cnu.edu.ph` Google account, not with Normalite EDGE. Contact CNU IT or your instructor. Normalite EDGE never sees your password, so nobody here can reset it.

### "I signed in but the system shows me nothing"

Your account has not been set up yet, or it has been disabled. Tell your instructor or administrator.

### "I forgot my password"

You do not have one. You sign in with Google. If you cannot get into your Google account, that is a Google problem — contact CNU IT.

### "I cannot see an exam my classmates can see"

Most likely your program is recorded incorrectly. Ask your instructor to check it.

Otherwise: the exam may not be open yet or may have closed, or you may have used all your attempts, or you may be inside a cooldown period.

### "I cannot see any exams at all"

Check with your instructor that exams have been published to your program. If your classmates can see them and you cannot, your program record is probably wrong.

### "My internet dropped during an exam"

Sign back in and resume. Your answers were saved up to the last automatic save, within the previous 15 seconds.

**Your time kept running while you were disconnected.** If time expired, the exam submitted automatically with what you had answered.

### "I ran out of time because my device failed"

Tell your instructor what happened, as soon as possible. Whether you are given another attempt is an academic decision for them — the system will not decide it for you. Report it promptly; a report made a week later is much harder for anyone to act on.

### "I was flagged for switching tabs but I did not cheat"

Tell your instructor and explain what happened. Notification pop-ups and system alerts can steal focus without you doing anything wrong, and instructors know this.

To avoid it next time, close everything else before starting.

### "The page says 'too many requests'"

A security limit that protects accounts from attack. Wait a few minutes and try again.

### "Something is broken"

Tell your instructor, and include: what you were doing, what you expected, what happened instead, roughly when, and whether it happens every time. That last part helps more than you would think.

**If anything went wrong during an exam, report it immediately** — do not wait until results come out.

## 11. How to actually use this to pass

The software is a tool. This is what the tool is for.

### The loop that works

```
   Study a deck  ──►  Take a mock exam  ──►  Read every explanation
        ▲                                              │
        │                                              │
        └──────  Make a deck of what you got wrong  ◄───┘
```

Round and round. Each pass, the deck of things you get wrong gets smaller — and that shrinking deck is the most accurate picture of your readiness you will get.

### Six things that make a real difference

1. **Answer the daily question every day.** Fifteen seconds. Consistency beats intensity.
2. **Read the explanations on questions you got right**, not just the ones you got wrong. Being right for the wrong reason is a gap that hides itself.
3. **Build a custom deck of your own mistakes.** This is the highest-value thing you can do in the system, and almost nobody does it.
4. **Take mock exams under real conditions** — full time limit, no interruptions, nothing else open. A mock exam taken casually measures nothing.
5. **Be honest in flashcard mode.** Answer before revealing. Recognition is not knowledge.
6. **Watch your trend, not single scores.** Direction matters more than any one result.

### And one thing worth remembering

The purpose of a mock exam is to find out what you do not know, while there is still time to fix it. A low score two months before the LET is useful information. A high score obtained by looking up answers is not information at all.

Find your gaps early. That is what this is for.

**Good luck.**
